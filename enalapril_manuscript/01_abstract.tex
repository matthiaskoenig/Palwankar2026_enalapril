\section*{Abstract}\label{sec:abstract}\justifying{}
\textbf{Background/Objectives:} Enalapril is an angiotensin-converting enzyme (ACE) inhibitor widely prescribed for hypertension and other cardiovascular conditions. It is a prodrug that is converted by carboxylesterase 1 (CES1) in the liver to the active metabolite enalaprilat, and both compounds are eliminated by the kidneys. Renal impairment, hepatic impairment and CES1 genetic variation are therefore expected to alter exposure to enalapril and enalaprilat. The aim of this work was to develop an open and reproducible physiologically based pharmacokinetic (PBPK) model of enalapril and enalaprilat and to use it to investigate the effect of these factors. \textbf{Methods:} The model was implemented in the Systems Biology Markup Language (SBML) and parameterised using a curated database of 49 clinical studies. Parameters were fitted in three steps (intravenous enalaprilat, oral enalapril and hepatic conversion) to single-dose data from healthy and hypertensive subjects. The model was then validated against independent data from multiple dosing, renal, hepatic and cardiac impairment, and CES1 variants. Parameter scans across renal function, liver function and CES1 activity were performed for a single oral dose. \textbf{Results:} The model reproduced enalapril and enalaprilat concentrations in serum, plasma and urine across a wide range of doses and study populations. Hepatic impairment increased enalapril exposure (AUC and t\textsubscript{1/2}) and reduced the formation and urinary excretion of enalaprilat. Renal impairment increased plasma concentrations of both compounds and reduced their urinary recovery. Reduced CES1 activity affected the conversion of enalapril to enalaprilat in the same direction as hepatic impairment, whereas increased activity had the opposite effect. \textbf{Conclusions:} The model provides a mechanistic basis for investigating the sources of variability in enalapril pharmacokinetics. The model is available in SBML under a CC-BY 4.0 licence, and all curated data are freely available from the PK-DB pharmacokinetics database.

Keywords: hypertension, enalapril, pharmacokinetics, ACE inhibitor, renal impairment, hepatic impairment, CES1
